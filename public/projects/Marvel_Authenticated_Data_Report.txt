AAKASH-JCJ25002859MBA-FINANCEGuided byMr. VISHNU PRASAD NAIDU Executive SummaryThis project traces Marvel’s journey from being a niche comic book publisher to becoming a billion-dollar global entertainment brand, analysing its financial growth and revenue evolution. In their initial years, the primary source of income was from comic book sales and limited merchandise sales. Still, the financial crisis within the company led it to rethink its business model. To create new revenue streams, Marvel started licensing their characters, but the real turning point came when its characters made their entry in the Marvel Cinematic Universe (MCU). Alongside films, Marvel widened its revenue streams through toys, merchandise, and video games. Also, Collaboration with theme parks and global distributors boosted its scale and visibility. In 2009, Marvel was acquired by Disney, which gave financial strength, enabling high-budget, interconnected films to reshape the blockbuster culture in Hollywood. With the most consistent box-office hit, Marvel expanded its revenue streams across films, streaming, comics, merchandising, licensing and collaborations. The rise of Disney into Disney+ strengthened the overseas market of Marvel movies, which contributed significant revenue to the company. Due to strong brand penetration fuelled by global demand, Marvel has become a multimedia empire, which is also helped in building a global community.IntroductionMarvel was started in 1939 as Timely Comics by Martin Goodman, which later evolved into Marvel Comics. The 1940s were the Golden Age of Marvel, which introduced Captain America by Joe Simon &amp; Jack Kirby. Also, Marvel had a great lineup with the characters Human Torch, Sub-Mariner and Captain America known as “Big Three”. During World War II, comics sales peaked with Adolf Hitler being punched by Captain America, along with the other comics. Comics served as the morale boosters and symbols of American idols. However, superheroes lost popularity after World War II ended. In the 1950s, Superheroes started disappearing, even the most famous, Captain America being discontinued in the 1950s. But by the end of the 1950s, Superheroes created a comeback wave due to DC comics' success with The Flash (1956) and Justice League of America (1960). In 1960, Publisher Martin Goodman asked editor Stan Lee to create a team of Superheroes to compete with DC’s Justice League. This led to Fantastic Four in 1961, which initiated the Marvel Universe and the Silver Age of Marvel. Marvel started expansion into various genres of social issues like racism, politics, feminism and drugs. Marvel was enjoying commercial success, but later, sales crashed due to the collector bubble burst because of overproduction (Oversaturation), failed distribution experiments, financial mismanagement and talent loss. After filing for Bankruptcy, Marvel survived by selling its licensing rights for characters to different studios, which earned the company small profits. The company entered the 2000s with movies such as X-Men (2000) and Spider-Man (2001), which slowly re-established Marvel. In 2008, Marvel finally initiated its first production film, Iron Man, marking the beginning of the Marvel Cinematic Universe (MCU), which revolutionised the filmmaking of interconnected stories. Marvel emerged as a global entertainment brand when Disney acquired it in 2009 for $4 billion. Marvel drowned in success with the movies Avengers: Infinity War (2018) and Avengers: Endgame (2019), collecting over $4.5 billion. Post-COVID period, Marvel expanded its storytelling to Television series such as Loki (2021) and WandaVision (2021) through the Disney+ platform.
